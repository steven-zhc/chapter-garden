At one in the morning, only the two of them were left in the observatory.

Su Mi stared at the crew manifest for a long time. At last she said, “I've never applied for a flight mission.”

Lin Shu believed her. But the employee number on the manifest was hers too, down to the last digit.

He went back to the recording and isolated the pulse in the background. It kept perfect time with the atomic clock, yet every so often it skipped a beat, as if someone were tapping out a string of numbers with silences. Su Mi wrote down where the gaps fell and converted them into an index. It pointed to Mirror Nine of the array.

“She's telling us which mirror the accident starts with,” Su Mi said.

Lin Shu pulled up Mirror Nine's commissioning log. Everything looked normal except for one command he had never seen before: in the ninth minute after activation, Mirror Nine would turn on its own and aim the gathered sunlight at a stretch of orbit on the far side of Earth. Nothing was there except the probe that had been out of service for seventeen years.

At the bottom of the log was a roster change made an hour earlier. The person riding the maintenance shuttle into orbit tomorrow morning for the final calibration was now Su Mi.

“No one told me,” she said.

They sent a report to the station director. Ten minutes later, he called back, sounding exhausted. “It's already seventy below in Antarctica. Without proof, I can't shut down the mirror array because of an echo.”

After he hung up, Su Mi was quiet for a while. Then she asked, “When does the next flare arrive?”

“In forty minutes.”

“Then we'll answer her in the ninth minute.”

They switched the radio array to transmit mode. When the flare reached the ground, the curve on the screen jumped. Eight minutes had passed since it left the sun. Lin Shu watched the atomic clock and counted off one more minute. In the very second the ninth minute ended, exactly one minute after the flare's arrival, when the echo had always come, he pressed send, and Su Mi spoke into the microphone. “Why are you on that probe?”

The echo came back faster than ever, almost on the heels of the ninth minute.

“Because when Mirror Nine turns, you're the only one beside it.” The future Su Mi paused, and her voice seemed to turn toward someone else. “Lin Shu, this time, don't let her go up.”
