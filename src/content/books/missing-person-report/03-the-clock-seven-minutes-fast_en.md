Xu Wen looked at his watch. 6:53. Over the phone, the station clock kept striking until it had struck seven.

“Your clock is seven minutes fast,” he said. “Don't turn around.”

“I'm not.” Her husband's voice had dropped to a whisper. “I can see her in the glass of the ticket hall. Gray scarf. Dark coat. Her left hand is on my shoulder.” He swallowed. “There's a tag hanging out of her sleeve.”

Shen Yanqiu looked down at the coat on the table, at the paper tag on its white thread. “What does her face look like?”

A long pause. “Like yours. But she isn't looking at me. She's looking at the clock.”

Xu Wen picked up his keys. Shen Yanqiu stood to follow him, and he stopped her with one hand. He tapped the report. *She will vanish at the station tomorrow night.* Tomorrow had become tonight.

“You stay here,” he said. “Whatever happens, you don't go near that station.”

He left her in the interview room with the duty officer and the open suitcase, and he kept the call going on the dashboard as he drove.

“She asked for a pen,” her husband said at a red light.

“Don't give it to her.”

“I already did. I always carry one.” Paper rustled. “She's writing on the back of my platform ticket.”

“Read it to me.”

“*Reporting person: Shen Yanqiu.*” He breathed out. “*Missing person*—she's still writing. She writes the way I do. She just wrote a one, changed it to a seven, and put a dot beside it.”

“What time did she write?”

“*Seven p.m.*”

Xu Wen ran the light.

He reached the station at 7:05 by his watch. The clock above the ticket hall said 7:12. Her husband stood alone by the glass, the pen still in his hand, the ticket held out in front of him as if someone had just let go of it.

Xu Wen took it. On the back, in the husband's handwriting: *Missing person: Shen Yanqiu. Last seen: the station, 7:00 p.m.* The dot beside the seven was small and very round.

“I didn't write that,” her husband said.

Xu Wen's phone buzzed. It was the duty officer, and he was talking too fast.

“She's gone. The interview room door opened by itself at seven by our clock. I was right there. She was talking to the empty chair, and then—” He stopped. “The suitcase is still here, and so is the scarf.”

“And the coat?”

The officer did not answer for a moment.

“The coat's gone,” he said. “The tag is on the floor.”
