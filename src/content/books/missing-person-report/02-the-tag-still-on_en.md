Xu Wen paused the footage. “Where did you buy it?”

“In Harbin. A small shop near the hotel, yesterday at noon.” Shen Yanqiu's voice was steady, but her hand had found the scar on her finger. “It's still in my suitcase. I haven't even cut the tag off.”

The suitcase stood by the door of the interview room. She knelt, turned the combination lock, and lifted the lid. The gray scarf lay folded on top. Beneath it was the coat: charcoal wool, horn buttons, a paper tag hanging from the left sleeve on a white plastic thread.

Xu Wen held the coat up beside the frozen frame. Same collar. Same buttons. In the reflection, the woman's left cuff sat slightly crooked, as if something were caught inside it.

He turned the sleeve over. The tag was there, exactly where the cuff would fold.

“So someone owns a second one,” Shen Yanqiu said.

“Maybe.” Xu Wen took out the receipt she had tucked into the pocket. The shop's name, the price, the last four digits of her card. And the time: 12:53.

Shen Yanqiu looked at it for a long while. “Noon,” she said at last. “Twelve fifty-three in the afternoon.”

“And twelve hours before you paid for it, a woman wearing this coat stood behind your husband outside a police station two thousand kilometers away.” He laid the receipt next to the report. On the form, the dot beside the corrected seven was small and very round. On the receipt, a pen had added the same dot beside the printed seven, as if checking it off.

“I didn't write that,” she said.

“Did your husband know you'd be in Harbin?”

“He booked the trip.” She stopped. “He told me to buy something warm.”

Xu Wen asked her to call him. She put the phone on speaker. It rang four times, and then her husband answered over the rumble of an announcement.

“Where are you?” she asked.

“The station. Your train gets in at seven.” He sounded cheerful and a little out of breath. “I'm early. I've been watching the clock.”

Shen Yanqiu stared at the suitcase, at the airline tag still looped around its handle.

“I didn't take the train,” she said. “I flew home this afternoon.”

The line went quiet. Behind him, the announcement ended, and a station clock began to strike—once, then again, though by Xu Wen's watch it was not yet the hour.

“Then who,” her husband asked softly, “is standing next to me?”
