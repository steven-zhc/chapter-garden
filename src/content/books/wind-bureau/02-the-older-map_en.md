Gu Zhiwei knew the name: Shen Yanqiu.

She had never met Shen Yanqiu. But every newcomer to the modern weather station saw her photo on the corridor wall. Thirty years ago, on a typhoon night, Shen Yanqiu had stayed alone in the duty room, and by morning she was gone. Gu Zhiwei had copied out an entire volume of her duty logs as an intern. She even remembered how Shen always flicked the last stroke of the character for *water* upward.

The writing on the older map had that same stroke.

The area around the south gate was covered in fine lines, and every lane was marked with its elevation—almost the same numbers Gu Zhiwei had just written on the back of the forecast.

At the very bottom of the map was a line in even smaller characters:

**If you came from there too, remember: they don't believe in rain. They only believe in things that carry a seal.**

Gu Zhiwei looked up. The chief official was staring at the older map, his face paler than before.

“Thirty years ago, you were here?” she asked.

He was silent for a long time. “Back then I was a junior clerk here. She said there would be rain. The governor said that day belonged to the river god's sacrifice, and rain was not permitted. I stamped the seal.”

Someone outside shouted again: the crack in the embankment had widened by another finger's breadth.

Gu Zhiwei picked up the official forecast that said **Clear skies** and turned it face up. The seal was real. The words were real. And the rain at late afternoon tomorrow would be real too.

“Can a forecast from the Bureau of Winds be changed?”

“Once it carries the seal, no.”

“Then we won't change it.” She added one more line to the back of the forecast. “This says clear skies tomorrow. It doesn't say no one may move house tonight. Stamp it again—on the back.”

The official looked at her, holding the seal in the air for a long time.

At last, it came down beside the evacuation routes.

Gu Zhiwei rolled up the older map and walked toward the door. She suddenly remembered that Shen Yanqiu's log from the night she vanished stopped halfway. The last line read: **People at the south gate: go to the City God Temple first.**

Thirty years ago, no one had followed it.

Tonight, she would make more than four hundred families walk that route.
