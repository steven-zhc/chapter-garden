The second hand on the clock began to move.

Zhou Mian had never noticed it before. Now it ticked past the six, loud as a knuckle on glass, and the printer fell silent to listen.

“Thirty seconds,” said the voice on the phone.

She tried to lift her hand. The woman's card came up with it. So did the man's. The two pieces of plastic clung to her palm as if they had grown there, the receipt pressed flat between them.

“You see,” the man said. He had not moved from the counter. His gray hand rested beside the keyboard, patient. “You are holding two. You can give me one.”

*Don't give the card to the person in the elevator.*

Zhou Mian did not answer him. Refusing was an answer. She looked at the screen instead, at the first record for Room Zero. Guest name: Zhou Mian. Arrival: October 22. Departure: October 23, 3:17 a.m.

The dates were right. Her manager had only ever told her one thing, and it had never been *don't accept the card*. It had been *verify the date first*.

“Twenty,” said the voice.

She peeled the woman's card off her palm with her other hand. It came away with a sound like wet paper tearing. She checked the date on the screen one more time, said it aloud—“October twenty-third, three-seventeen”—and slid the card into the reader.

The system chimed. *Checkout complete.*

The first record closed. Outside the glass door, the woman under the streetlight lowered her arm. For a moment Zhou Mian thought she smiled. Then the streetlight flickered, and the pavement where she had stood was only wet.

The man's card was still stuck to Zhou Mian's hand. One card. Only one.

The second hand reached the twelve.

3:18.

Nothing happened. The man looked at the clock, then at her, and for the first time his politeness slipped. “That was hers to pay,” he said.

“She checked out,” Zhou Mian said. “The dates matched.”

The printer started again. A new receipt crept out, line by line. Arrival: October 23, 3:18 a.m. Room: 0. Guest name: blank. Under *payment method*, the same four words.

**The next guest pays.**

Behind her the elevator chimed. She turned. On the panel inside, below the button for the first floor, a button she had never seen was lit.

“Don't get in,” said the voice on the phone, very quietly. “And don't let the next guest in. It's already 3:18 where I am, and someone is ringing the bell.”
