Zhou Mian did not look up.

She kept her eyes on the screen and listened. The footsteps that left the elevator were dry, unhurried, and stopped exactly where the wet prints ended. A second key card slid across the counter and came to rest beside the first. It too had a white **0** printed on it.

“Checking out,” said a man's voice, polite and very close.

Her manager's instruction was the only rule she had. *Verify the date before accepting the key card.* She did not touch the card. She typed the room number instead.

The system paused, then opened a second record for Room Zero. Guest name: blank. Arrival: October 23. Departure: October 22.

The arrival came after the departure.

“The dates are wrong,” Zhou Mian said. “I can't accept this card.”

“Then I'll take hers.” A gray hand, dry as paper, moved toward the woman's card. Zhou Mian slapped her palm down over it. The receipt she was still holding crackled between her fingers and the plastic.

Her phone rang.

The caller ID showed her own name. This time she answered.

The voice on the line was hoarse and sounded as if it were speaking through water, but it was hers. “Has it asked for the card yet? Don't refuse it. Refusing is an answer. Ask it when it arrived.”

Zhou Mian swallowed. “When did you arrive?”

The hand stopped. For a long moment the lobby was silent except for the hum of the printer.

“The day you pay,” the man said.

The receipt in her fist grew warm. She opened her hand. The words under *payment method* were still there, **The next guest pays**, but now a line of small print had appeared beneath them: *Guest on duty: Zhou Mian.*

Outside the glass front door, the soaked woman had not left. She stood under the streetlight with water running from her hair and pointed at the clock above the desk.

It read 3:17. It had read 3:17 since the bell rang.

“Look at the clock,” the voice on the phone said. “When it turns 3:18, whoever is holding two cards checks in.”

Zhou Mian looked down. Her palm was still pressed over the woman's card. And somehow, without her feeling it, the man's card had slipped under her fingers too.
