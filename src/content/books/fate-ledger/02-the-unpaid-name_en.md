The instant Xie Wujiu's red brush touched the page, the funeral bell sounded again below.

A fourth stroke.

The junior clerk froze. The Hall of Destinies sounded its bell only three times, unless the dead person's name had yet to be settled.

Wujiu opened his mother's ledger. *To die tomorrow* was still there. Beside it, a line of writing as fine as stitches had appeared: **Rejected. Settle the original debt.** The words he had just written, *I forbid it*, turned bright red. Vermilion seeped through the page onto the desk and ran along the grain toward his own ledger.

He pushed the old volume away, but the ink stopped at the seventh deferral slip. His mother's signature was still wet. On the sixth slip beneath it, the blurred strokes sharpened into another name.

“Chen Aman?” the clerk read aloud. “The old woman who sold medicine down the mountain? She was buried last month.”

Wujiu remembered her. Every year at first frost, she brought the Hall a packet of ointment for chilblains, saying his mother had asked her to deliver it. Last year, her left hand had been wrapped in cloth, and the right had trembled around the packet. He had never asked why.

He lifted the sixth slip. The fifth bore another name: the ferryman killed in a flood. The slips before it named people he had known as well: the teacher who taught him his characters, a courier who had carried letters for his mother, and someone whose childhood nickname he thought he had forgotten.

The signatures had not faded with age. Wherever the vermilion reached, their real names came into view.

“Don't touch it,” Wujiu told the clerk, stopping his hand above the pages. His mother's signature on the seventh slip was still wet. Beneath the ink was a blank space just wide enough for Wujiu's thumb.

Footsteps sounded on the stairs. The old keeper of the ledgers came up carrying a lamp. At the sight of the vermilion on the desk, he looked first at Wujiu's hand, then toward the bell.

“You wrote *I forbid it*?” The keeper set his lamp at a distance. “Only the one who signs can pay for an order that overturns a death. Your mother's year has not run out. Put your print there for her, and tomorrow the ledger will come for you.”

“And if I don't?”

“At the fifth stroke, her name is settled.”

Wind moved beneath the eaves. Wujiu heard the bell's tongue brush its rim. He thought of the cloth around Chen Aman's hand and asked, “Did the first six agree to this?”

The keeper did not answer. His gaze had fallen on the first page of Wujiu's ledger, and the color left his face.

The page had once said: *Died at sixteen, after falling into a well.* Now the words *falling into a well* were vanishing. Under them, an older line in red slowly emerged: **At sixteen, took up the brush and altered the ledger.**

Wujiu looked up. The keeper was already reaching to pinch out the lamp.

The bell sounded a fifth time.
